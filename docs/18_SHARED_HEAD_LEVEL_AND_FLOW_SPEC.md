# 18 — Shared-Head Level & Flow Spec

## Core layout

One central chair on a small clear work platform.

Around it:
- 360-degree walking access,
- enough room for four bodies,
- tool racks within short running distance,
- mirrors on walls,
- validation prop area,
- no dense decorative collision immediately around the chair.

Four colored approach zones may be used for spawn/readability, but they are not owned territories.

## Tool placement philosophy

Do not give players every tool in an instant inventory.

Physical placement creates useful behavior:
- running to grab a tool,
- noticing what a teammate picked up,
- dropping a dangerous tool,
- competing for workspace rather than abstract inventory slots.

Keep frequently used safe tool(s) close to center. Place dangerous/special tools slightly farther away or on distinct racks so their appearance is readable.

## Shared goal display

Use a large readable goal preview visible from the work area plus minimal HUD reminder.

Avoid text-heavy instructions. The physical validator should teach the target whenever possible.

## Validator staging

Props can sit in a clearly readable "validation" zone before activation:
- helicopter,
- eggs,
- cat,
- rocket,
- toy vehicle.

This foreshadows the final test and gives players something funny to glance at.

## Customer entrance / replacement

Prototype can simply swap/reset customer between jobs. Do not build a full queue system yet.

## Mirrors

Mirrors are valuable for:
- seeing teammates behind you,
- seeing your own damaged hair,
- observing chaos without switching camera.

Use the cheapest acceptable implementation; perfect recursive rendering is not required if expensive.
